Descriptive Statistics for Data Analysis

Descriptive statistics summarize and describe the main features of a dataset without drawing conclusions beyond the data itself.

Measures of central tendency:

Mean is the average of all values, calculated by summing them and dividing by the count. It is sensitive to outliers.

Median is the middle value when data is sorted. It is more robust to outliers than the mean.

Mode is the most frequently occurring value in the dataset. It is useful for categorical data.

Measures of spread:

Range is the difference between the maximum and minimum values.

Variance measures how far each value in the dataset is from the mean, on average, squared.

Standard deviation is the square root of the variance, expressed in the same units as the original data. A low standard deviation means values are close to the mean; a high one means values are spread out.

Interquartile Range (IQR) is the difference between the 75th percentile (Q3) and the 25th percentile (Q1). It represents the spread of the middle 50% of the data.

Outliers are values that fall unusually far from the rest of the data. A common rule using IQR is: a value is an outlier if it is below Q1 - 1.5*IQR or above Q3 + 1.5*IQR.

Correlation:

Correlation measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two numerical variables, expressed as a value between -1 and 1.

A correlation close to 1 means a strong positive relationship (both variables increase together).

A correlation close to -1 means a strong negative relationship (one increases as the other decreases).

A correlation close to 0 means little to no linear relationship.

Correlation does not imply causation: two variables can be correlated without one causing the other.

Skewness:

Skewness describes the asymmetry of a distribution. Positive skew means a longer tail on the right (most values are low, with some high outliers). Negative skew means a longer tail on the left.

Missing data:

Missing values can be handled by removing rows/columns with too many missing values, filling them with the mean/median/mode (imputation), or using a placeholder value, depending on how much data is missing and why.
